Why test this?

The official list is the right starting point. It does not answer every job question.

Oregon Health Authority maintains a public list of certified Backflow Assembly Testers. That list is voluntary and includes certification and submitted business-license information.

This pilot tests the work that still remains: who serves your area, handles your type of property and work, uses your utility's reporting process, has time before your deadline, and is willing to respond.

A small manual test, not a finished marketplace

    Send the basics

    Tell us the location, utility if known, type of work, and timing. Do not include sensitive account information.

Before an introduction

Separate the facts that directories often blend together

  • Service fitDoes the provider state that it serves the county, property type, and work requested?
  • Utility processDoes the provider state that it can file through the required reporting method?
  • AvailabilityCan the provider respond within the buyer's timing?

Questions

What this pilot does and does not do

Does a match mean the provider is approved or recommended?

No. A match means the available information appears relevant to the request. The pilot does not certify, approve, endorse, employ, or guarantee providers. Confirm requirements with your water utility and provider before hiring.

Is the service affiliated with Oregon Health Authority?

No. It is an independent research pilot. OHA's public list is one source used to check certification information.

Will my contact details be shared?

The buyer form asks you to choose. If you consent, the request may be shared with plausible matched providers. If you do not consent, we can send public-source options without forwarding your contact details.

What if my deadline is urgent or water service is at risk?

Contact your water provider directly. This pilot does not provide emergency, legal, plumbing, or regulatory advice and cannot guarantee a response time.
